
Was this a problem for llama.cpp as well
Potentially, yes—but not necessarily in the same way.

Your previous llama.cpp benchmarks used:

-lm mlock -lzm off
-ot 'engram_embd=CPU;exps=CPU'

The important distinction is that -lm mlock requests memory locking; it does not raise the process's RLIMIT_MEMLOCK limit.

Before we fixed the container, that limit was only 8 MiB. If llama.cpp attempted to lock hundreds of gigabytes, those requests could not have succeeded under that limit.

That means our earlier assumption that the entire model was locked in RAM was not adequately verified.

However, failing to lock memory does not mean the model was necessarily being read from SSD. The Linux page cache can retain the GGUF files in RAM without mlock, particularly on your 2 TiB server.

I checked llama.cpp's implementation. When an mlock() call fails, llama.cpp logs a warning and continues rather than necessarily aborting.
