What this suggests
The 32 GiB cache may be creating enough expert misses, transfers, or replacement overhead to outweigh its benefits. At 64 GiB, more accesses can be satisfied directly from VRAM, producing a net improvement.

The near-identical prefill numbers are also significant. They suggest that this cache-size adjustment is not addressing llama.cpp's primary prompt-processing bottleneck.

My recommendation
Stop reducing the cache. On this configuration, 64 GiB is demonstrably better than 32 GiB.

We now have evidence of an important distinction between Maya and llamda:

Maya uses an expert-frequency profile to populate VRAM and adapt its residency decisions.

llamda's dynamic cache appears sensitive to capacity, and at 32 GiB its overhead exceeds the benefit.

Maya's specialized execution path achieves substantially greater undrafted throughput, although the benchmark workloads differ.

For future llamda experiments, I would retain 64 GiB as the baseline and investigate cache hit rates, PCIe traffic, and GPU expert execution. A 48–60 GiB sweep could help locate the cache's useful capacity threshold, but it is unlikely by itself to close the gap with Maya.

Meanwhile, I would proceed with the GLM-5.3-Flash Maya-L benchmark. It will help establish whether Maya's advantage extends beyond DeepSeek-V4.1 and give us a second architecture for comparison.
